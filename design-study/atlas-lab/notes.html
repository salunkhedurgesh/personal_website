Homepage sequence

A guided first visit.

  1. Who you are.

    Name, portrait, role, a short research introduction, and a compact background line. The person comes before any technical result.

  2. What you work on.

    Three readable topics: kinematic intelligence; geometry and motion planning; mechanism design. Each opens the Research page at the relevant theme.

  3. Recent work worth exploring.

    Science Robotics 2026 leads with a simple question and opens a dedicated research story. A second result points into the geometry theme. Readers can continue scrolling without taking either detour.

  4. What you’re working on now.

    Specific investigations and collaborators link to Projects. Current project wording comes from the existing site and needs a status check before launch.

  5. Where to learn or get in touch.

    Courses have their own page. The homepage offers a short invitation and shows how a dark simulation space could fit into a light site. A past-students section can follow current projects later, once real entries are ready.

Color experiments

Black. White. One accent.

Open the controls at the top of the page. Each swatch changes the same layout, making the comparison about color rather than different content.

The three base colors define the site chrome. Gray is derived from black; hover effects may blend the chosen accent with white. The portrait is displayed in grayscale through CSS. Simulation images retain their meaningful colors on a fixed dark canvas.

Button text switches between black and white according to calculated contrast. Body copy, ordinary links, and focus indicators stay dark on white. This follows the principle behind WCAG text contrast; a pale accent does not have to do the job of readable text or a control boundary.

Typography experiments

Same name. Four voices.

Inter

Clear and unobtrusive.

The closest continuation of Atlas. Works well when diagrams, writing, and projects should carry the identity.

Space Grotesk

A little more character.

A geometric choice that gives your name a more distinctive shape. Try it with neon lime and compare the smaller research descriptions too.

IBM Plex Sans

A technical, human voice.

An alternative for an academic site with plenty of teaching and technical content. Try it with blue or pastel mint.

Newsreader + Inter

An editorial emphasis.

Serif headings bring some of Field Notes’ warmth into Atlas’s structure. Inter keeps navigation and body copy consistent. Try pastel lilac or peach.

All four fonts are served locally in the preview. Licenses and official source URLs are recorded in font sources. These are aesthetic directions to compare, not a ranking of the font families.

Effects experiments

Enough response to feel alive.

Quiet

Just the essentials.

Static layout with clear hover and focus states. The baseline for checking whether an effect improves the experience.

Lift

A tactile response.

Cards move a few pixels; buttons gain a small shadow. Applied only on hover-capable devices, with no scroll-triggered content hiding.

Spotlight

A trace of the accent.

A low-intensity glow follows the pointer inside a card. It uses only the selected accent. Keyboard focus gets a static highlight.

Effects provide no essential information and are disabled for reduced-motion preferences. The preview does not add a working simulation; dark examples establish a direction for that later phase.
