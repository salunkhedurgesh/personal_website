01 / Kinematic intelligence

Can a skill travel
between different robots?

A shared task does not mean a shared body. Link lengths, joint limits and configurations change what each robot can do.

The research direction

Teach the task. Account for the robot.

A demonstration describes the desired behavior. Kinematic analysis describes the robot’s available motion. I work on connecting these two descriptions so learning can use a robot’s geometry from the beginning.

Learning from demonstration · Dynamical systems · Skill transfer

For a first encounter

Picture the same gesture, with a different arm.

If an arm has shorter links or tighter joint limits, copying another robot’s joint angles will not reproduce the same motion. What should transfer—and what should adapt?

02 / Geometry & motion planning

Reaching a point is only
the beginning.

A robot may reach every point on a path and still struggle to follow the entire path from its starting configuration.

The research direction

Understand the space of possible motions.

My work studies inverse kinematic solutions, singularities and cuspidality: the ability of certain robots to change inverse kinematic solutions without crossing a singularity. This geometry has consequences for path feasibility, repeatability and robot design.

Inverse kinematics · Cuspidality · Redundant manipulators

03 / Mechanism design

What can the mechanism
itself make possible?

Geometry is also a design choice. Changing a joint or an architecture changes the capabilities available to learning and control.

The research direction

Design useful motion into the robot.

I investigate robot architectures and mechanisms with attention to mobility, actuation and compliance. This connects earlier work on parallel manipulators and surgical mechanisms with newer work on mechanically intelligent joints.

Parallel mechanisms · Kinematic design · Novel joints
