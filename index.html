I believe,

“Pure learning is powerful but blind. Pure analysis is precise but brittle. The future of robotics demands both — robots that are mechanically intelligent by design, and relentlessly adaptive by learning.”

Demonstrate once, execute on many: Kinematic intelligence for cross-robot skill transfer

Learning from demonstration (LfD) enables intuitive skill acquisition, but learned policies historically fail when transferred to robots with different kinematic structures or limits. We embed analytical singularities and joint constraints directly into globally stable dynamical systems, achieving zero-shot cross-robot transfer across both redundant and non-redundant manipulators.

Authors: Sthithpragya Gupta, Durgesh Haribhau Salunkhe, Aude Billard

PDF DOI Link BibTeX
Teaching robots new skills should be as natural as showing rather than programming. Learning from demonstration (LfD) moves toward this goal by allowing users to guide a robot or sketch a desired motion, enabling learning without writing a line of code. However, most LfD methods remain tied to the robot they were trained on. Here, we introduce a framework that endows robots with kinematic intelligence: an internal understanding of their own joint limits, singularities, and connectivity.

Cuspidal Redundant Robots: Classification of Infinitely Many IKS of Special Classes of 7R Robots

Redundant 7-DOF robots offer superior dexterity but introduce complex kinematic issues: cuspidality allows them to transition between inverse kinematic solutions without meeting a singularity. Under the European DARKO initiative, we established a systematic framework reducing 7R architectures to parameterized 3R equivalents, proving when commercial cobots exhibit unexpected nonsingular posture changes.

Authors: Durgesh Haribhau Salunkhe, Sthithpragya Gupta, Aude Billard
